This article provides a step by step guide to comparing the new Gemini 4 models with Gemma 4 on equal terms. Seven models, Gemma 4 26B and 31B, Gemini 3.5 Flash-Lite and 3.8 Flash, and Gemini 4 Hydrogen, Carbon and Argon, run through the same Gemini API endpoint with the same questions, settings and scoring, started at the same moment.

https://github.com/xbill9/gemma4-dev/tree/main/gemini-api-google-hydrogen4

The scores: on 1,319 GSM8K math problems Argon scored 0.9841, Hydrogen and Carbon 0.9803, 3.8 Flash 0.9788, Gemma 4 26B 0.9742, Gemma 4 31B 0.9735 and 3.5 Flash-Lite 0.9629. On 400 BFCL function calls Argon and Gemma 4 31B tied at 0.9675. Gemma 4 ran free; the Gemini runs cost $0.92 to $5.02. Hydrogen answered fastest of the Gemini 4 models, 2.84 s per GSM8K problem against 7.63 s for Argon.

The surprise is in the cost, and it takes the whole picture to see it. Hydrogen is the cheapest Gemini 4 model per token, $7.50 per million output tokens against $10 for Carbon and Argon, and it cost the most per run, $5.02 against $3.46 for Argon. Its thinking eats the token budget: Google's docs say reasoning cannot be turned off on Gemini 4 models, it ran on all 1,319 GSM8K problems at the lowest setting, and it was 55% of the GSM8K bill. Price per token, tokens per answer and thinking per answer together set what a model costs.

What Is the Same for Every Model

Every model got:

  • the same endpoint, /v1beta/openai/chat/completions on the Gemini API
  • the same 1,319 GSM8K and 400 BFCL simple questions, prompts and scoring code
  • temperature 0, up to 2,048 tokens per GSM8K answer and 512 per BFCL answer
  • eight requests at a time, a 180 s limit per request, up to eight retries
  • the same start time: all seven began at 09:11:16 on 2026-11-14
  • thinking set as low as each model accepts

Step 2 shows that setting for each model, and the two models that keep thinking at it.

At This Point You Should Have…

  • A Gemini API key from Google AI Studio, saved in ~/gemini.key
  • Python 3 (the scoring script uses only the standard library)
  • A clone of gemma4-dev

Step 1 — The Endpoint

The Gemini API answers OpenAI chat requests with the key as a Bearer token. Gemma 4 and Gemini take the same request body; only model changes:

curl -sS https://generativelanguage.googleapis.com/v1beta/openai/chat/completions \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer $(cat ~/gemini.key)" -H 'Content-Type: application/json' \
  -d '{"model":"gemma-4-31b-it","temperature":0,"max_tokens":2048,"reasoning_effort":"minimal",
       "messages":[{"role":"user","content":"Janet has 16 eggs, eats 3, bakes with 4, sells the rest at $2. How much does she make? End with Answer: <number>"}]}'

The reply ends Answer: 18.

Google documents this. The same page documents none for 2.5 models only. On Hydrogen, 3.8 Flash, Carbon and Argon the endpoint accepts none and treats it as low. 🔎 Tip: Thinking Tokens Are Not in completion_tokens

On this endpoint completion_tokens counts only the visible answer. Thinking is total_tokens - prompt_tokens - completion_tokens, and Google bills it as output. The scoring script records it as reasoning_tokens on every row.

Step 7 — Where the Money Goes

By list price Hydrogen is the cheapest Gemini 4 model, $7.50 per million output tokens against $10 for Carbon and Argon. It cost the most because it thought on all 1,319 GSM8K problems at its lowest setting, 248.7 thinking tokens each on average, which made thinking 55% of its GSM8K bill, against 11% for Carbon and 2% for Argon. Argon thought on 66 problems and scored the highest.

The Gemini 4 models also count more input tokens for the same text: the GSM8K prompts are 160,432 tokens to Hydrogen, Carbon and Argon and 120,902 to Gemma 4 31B, Flash-Lite and 3.8 Flash, a mean of 121.6 against 91.7 per problem.

Thinking also shares the output limit with the answer. Google's Hydrogen page says max_output_tokens "caps combined thinking and output tokens". At this run's limits of 2,048 and 512 tokens, thinking used the limit before the answer arrived on 4 GSM8K problems and 2 BFCL calls for 3.8 Flash and on 5 GSM8K problems for Carbon; all 11 scored wrong. Sent again with a 16,384-token limit (cut-rows.py), 2 of the 4 on 3.8 Flash and 3 of the 5 on Carbon came back right. Hydrogen had no cut answers.

Compare and Contrast

What the API Makes Easy to Miss

Google documents both facts behind Hydrogen's bill: reasoning cannot be turned off on Gemini 4 models, and output prices include thinking tokens. Four things in the API still make the bill easy to misread:

  • Off-looking settings are accepted. reasoning_effort: "none" on the OpenAI endpoint and thinkingBudget: 0 on the native API return no error on Hydrogen. none runs as low, and nothing in the reply says thinking is still on.
  • Not setting it costs more. Hydrogen's default is high: 311.8 thinking tokens per problem on the 100-problem sample against 239.0 at none.
  • completion_tokens leaves thinking out. A client that reads it sees the visible answer only; on Hydrogen thinking was 55% of the GSM8K bill.
  • The per-token price invites the wrong comparison. $7.50 against $10 per million output tokens puts Hydrogen below Carbon and Argon; per run it cost 45% more than Argon, $5.02 against $3.46.

So, Which One?

For the best scores on short math problems and single function calls with thinking at its lowest setting, Gemini 4 Argon: highest on GSM8K, tied for highest on BFCL, and the cheapest of the three Gemini 4 models per run because it rarely thinks.

For hard, multi-step work, Google points to Carbon. Its Hydrogen page calls Carbon "our strongest frontier model", one that "spends more thinking tokens per turn to test hypotheses and verify edge cases on hard architecture, debugging, science, and security audits", and suggests pairing the two: Carbon plans and resolves hard roadblocks, Hydrogen does the code edits, subagent tasks and test runs. This run held Carbon to its lowest thinking setting, where it thought on 268 of 1,319 GSM8K problems, so that case is untested here. Five of its GSM8K answers were cut at the 2,048-token limit; with a 16,384-token limit 3 of them came back right, which still leaves it below Argon.

Gemini 4 Hydrogen fits four cases:

  • On the free tier. Hydrogen has one and Carbon and Argon do not. On free quota its thinking costs nothing, and it is the fastest Gemini 4 model, at 0.9803 on GSM8K like Carbon.
  • When response time matters more than cost. 2.84 s per GSM8K problem against 7.34 s for Carbon and 7.63 s for Argon; 620.8 s for the whole set against 1,759.2 s for Argon.
  • From 2026-12-01. Carbon and Argon go to $4 / $20. At those prices this run costs $6.93 on Argon and $8.03 on Carbon against $5.02 on Hydrogen, which makes Hydrogen the cheapest Gemini 4 model per run. That is arithmetic from list prices.
  • On hard coding and agent work. Google's thinking page recommends Hydrogen's default high level "for most tasks, including complex coding, agentic execution, tool calling". This run measured short math problems and single function calls, so that case is untested here.

For high-volume simple work on a paid tier, Gemini 3.5 Flash-Lite: $0.92 for both tests, no thinking, 1.10 s per problem, at the cost of being behind Gemma 4 31B on both tests.

For tool calling at no cost, Gemma 4 31B: tied with Argon on BFCL, 0.0106 behind it on GSM8K, free on the Gemini API, and the same weights run on your own hardware.

Summary

The goal of this article was to compare the new Gemini 4 models with Gemma 4 on equal terms. The key to the solution was running all seven models through one Gemini API endpoint, started together, with the same questions, scoring script and settings, with thinking set as low as each model accepts. The results were:

  • 🟢 Gemini 4 Argon scored highest: 0.9841 on GSM8K and 0.9675 on BFCL simple, for $3.46.
  • 🟢 Gemma 4 31B tied Argon on BFCL at 0.9675 and scored 0.9735 on GSM8K, on the free tier.
  • 🟢 Hydrogen, Carbon and Argon were ahead of Gemma 4 31B on GSM8K; on BFCL all three were level with it.
  • 🟢 Gemma 4 26B was level with the 31B on both tests.
  • 🟢 Hydrogen was the fastest Gemini 4 model, 2.84 s per GSM8K problem against 7.34 s for Carbon and 7.63 s for Argon.
  • ⚠️ Hydrogen has the lowest Gemini 4 price per token and cost the most per run, $5.02: thinking was 55% of its GSM8K bill.
  • ⚠️ Thinking cannot be turned off on Gemini 4 Hydrogen or 3.8 Flash, as Google's docs say. At the lowest setting Hydrogen thought on every problem and 3.8 Flash on 1,293 of 1,319, billed as output.
  • ⚠️ The API accepts none on Hydrogen without an error and runs it as low.
  • ⚠️ Thinking shares the output limit: it cut 6 answers on 3.8 Flash and 5 on Carbon at this run's limits.
  • 🟢 From 2026-12-01 Hydrogen becomes the cheapest Gemini 4 model per run, $5.02 against $6.93 for Argon, by arithmetic from list prices.
  • ⚠️ Thinking tokens are missing from completion_tokens on the OpenAI-compatible endpoint.
  • ⚠️ Carbon and Argon have no free tier and double in price on 2026-12-01.
  • ❌ Gemini 3.5 Flash-Lite and 3.8 Flash were behind Gemma 4 31B on BFCL.

Scope: one laptop in the US calling the Gemini API on 2026-11-14, one run per model, all seven started together. Thinking was set as low as each model accepts: minimal for Gemma 4 and Flash-Lite, none for the rest, which matched low on a 100-problem sample. The native thinking controls were tried on one question. The scores for 3.8 Flash and Carbon include the 11 answers cut by thinking at the 2,048 and 512 token limits. Three Gemma 4 requests that hit the free tier's rate limit were sent again with the same settings. Costs are token counts times list prices. Run times are from one run with all seven sharing one connection, and will move with load on the service. Hard multi-step tasks and higher thinking levels were not measured, which is the work Google positions Carbon for. Google does not say what hardware or engine serves any of these models.
