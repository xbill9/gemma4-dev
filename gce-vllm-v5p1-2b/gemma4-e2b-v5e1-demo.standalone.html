Is a hotdog a sandwich?

This is one of the great, and surprisingly passionate, debates of the internet!

There is no single, universally agreed-upon answer. Whether a hotdog is a sandwich depends entirely on the definition you choose to use.

Arguments for “Yes, it is a sandwich”

  1. The Basic Structure: A sandwich is generally defined as a filling served between, or enclosed by, slices of bread or a split roll. A hotdog fits this structure.
  2. Functional Equivalence: It serves the same purpose — a portable, handheld meal combining protein and carbohydrates.
  3. The Open-Faced Precedent: If an open-faced sandwich counts, then a hotdog qualifies.

Arguments for “No, it is not”

  1. The “Two Separate Pieces” Rule: Purists argue a true sandwich requires two distinct slices of bread; a hotdog bun is one continuous piece.
  2. The “Casing” Argument: The bread acts as a vessel for the meat rather than two components holding a filling between them.
  3. Taxonomic Distinction: The specific nature of sausage and bun makes it its own category.

Conclusion

A hotdog is a type of food that occupies an ambiguous space, leading to endless, enjoyable philosophical arguments.

How much wood can a woodchuck chuck?

TTFT 0.206s
Throughput 214.1tok/s
Tokens 334

That’s a classic tongue-twister! There’s no single, scientifically proven answer to how much wood a woodchuck can chuck.

The literal answer: since woodchucks (groundhogs) are burrowing animals, they don’t “chuck” wood in any measurable way.

The definitive (joke) answer:

A woodchuck would chuck as much wood as a woodchuck could chuck if a woodchuck could chuck wood.

The actual woodchuck metric: in the 1980s a wildlife biologist tried to calculate this, and estimated a woodchuck could move about 700 pounds of dirt when digging a burrow. But again — that’s dirt, not wood.

Read the numbers carefully

Single stream

Every figure here is one request at a time. 214 tok/s is single-stream decode throughput, not server capacity — concurrent throughput is far higher and needs a proper benchmark sweep to state.

The fp8 log line is misleading

vLLM logs Automatically using fp8_e5m2 for FP8 KV cache, but the actual allocation reports bfloat16, and the arithmetic agrees: 15 KiB per token across 15 layers over 1,151,744 tokens is 16.5 GiB. At fp8 the capacity would be roughly double.

Sliding layers are over-allocated

vLLM placed all 15 KV layers in a single cache group with identical block counts, so the 12 sliding-window layers — which never need more than 512 tokens of history — are each given the full 8,998 blocks. A window-aware allocator would free a large share of that 16.5 GiB. Inferred from the logs, not measured.

Ephemeral by construction

Flex-start capacity cannot be paused. The instance self-deletes at its 4-hour max-run-duration and the endpoint disappears with it.
