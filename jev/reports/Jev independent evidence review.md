# Independent tests recast Jev as a mid-tier classifier

Eight days after launch, the evidence that survives checking against raw outputs shows TypeSafe's Jev to be a fast, cheap zero-shot classifier whose accuracy on human-labelled data sits level with mid-size LLMs and several points behind the frontier, whose probabilities rank answers usefully but need refitting on each task's own labels, and whose speed and cost advantage ranges from parity to several hundred times depending on what is timed and priced. The largest pre-registered study (7,977 human-labelled items, [arXiv 2609.24574](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.24574)) finds Jev behind the best LLM on 14 of 15 tasks by a median 11.6 macro-F1 points, and eight independent Banking77 runs put it at 0.753–0.840 (median 0.809), below the best LLM run beside it in every study that ran one. TypeSafe's headline claims hold up unevenly: the 193.6x/444.6x multiples come from four in-house workflows scored against the averaged answers of two frontier LLMs with no stated comparator, "0% hallucination" is, in TypeSafe's own words, "not empirical", RLCD is an unpublished training target with no method attached, and the founder's "co-invented RLHF" rests on primary co-authorship of InstructGPT; he is not an author of the 2017 paper that introduced RLHF. Every piece of the mechanism (reading label-token logits, verbalizers, temperature scaling, cascades) was published between 2007 and 2023, and every trained open alternative is built on Qwen or an encoder and evaluated mainly by its own author or a commercial rival. For Gemma, the cell this project cares about, the literature holds five generative comparisons and one unreproducible DiffusionGemma demo; plain Gemma read by label logits, with calibration measured, is unpublished.

## Three verification levels separate re-scored numbers from quoted ones

This review draws on primary sources read on 2026-09-23: TypeSafe's own pages and docs, 14 arXiv preprints read in full, 104 GitHub evaluation repos audited block by block, 33 dev.to and Medium posts, and press and forum coverage. Each number below carries one of three levels of support. **Re-scored** means the figure was recomputed from committed per-item outputs and matched. **Checked** means the source's own arithmetic was recomputed from its published tables but per-item data is unavailable. **Reported** means the figure is the author's statement and cannot be checked from outside. Where the project's working synthesis (`jev/NOTES.md`) and the primary-source audits disagree, this review follows the audits and says so in the section on corrections.

The wider corpus is thinner than its volume suggests. Of 33 dev.to and Medium posts read in full, 5 are careful measurements on human or public labels, 11 are small, synthetic or model-scored, and 17 restate vendor claims. Of the 104 GitHub repos with Jev measurements, 33 grade A, 57 B, 5 C, and 9 are tools rather than evaluations. Nearly every grade-A headline re-scored exactly from committed files. Of the 14 arXiv preprints, **10 call TypeSafe's hosted Jev**, all on version 1.13, none carries a conflict-of-interest statement, and two of the ten (2609.22753 and 2609.23136) come from the same five UTS authors and count as one lab.

## On human labels Jev lands level with mid-size LLMs and behind the frontier

The two strongest independent studies agree on the shape of Jev's accuracy. In Ibrahim and Zaki's replication of the Ziems et al. social-science annotation suite, Jev (`jev-1.13-20260917` through OpenRouter) trails the per-task best of 19 LLMs on **14 of 15 tasks by a median 11.6 macro-F1 points** (recomputed 11.7), leading only on dialect, and the deficit survives false-discovery correction on 12 tasks ([2609.24574](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.24574)). Within Jev's own price band, Gemma 4 31B (0.611) and Qwen3 235B (0.604) beat Jev's median F1 of 0.581. Janardhan's 200-item, six-model run with committed logs gives the same ordering at small scale: **Jev 72.5% (95% CI 66–78.5)** against Fable 5.1 84.0, GPT-6 Astra 79.0, DeepSeek 76.0, MiniMax 75.5 and Kimi 74.5; the leads of the two frontier models (+11.5 and +6.5 points) have intervals that exclude zero, while the three mid-size models sit within noise of Jev ([jev-frontier-bench](https://github.com/manjunathshiva/jev-frontier-bench/blob/da39111cd5067c47162ca71f8d6bfec9328c8d36/README.md)).

Application studies place Jev closer to the frontier on narrower, binary tasks. On 195,857 Texas crash narratives coded against three blinded human coders, Jev's F1 is 0.908, statistically level with GPT-5.6 Sol (0.885) and 0.059 behind Fable 5.1 (0.967), though four rare variables fall below F1 0.70 (unbelted 0.45, intentional 0.49, wrong way 0.52, vehicle defect 0.69) and the human sample was drawn by stratifying on Jev's own probabilities ([2609.24052](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.24052)). As a judge, Jev is within 1.3 points of GPT-6 Astra on RewardBench and 0.8 ahead on HaluEval but **14.6 points behind on JudgeBench**, with the gap concentrated in reasoning (68.4 vs 95.9) and coding (76.2 vs 97.6) ([2609.26550](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.26550)).

Banking77, the one task many authors ran independently, gives the tightest estimate. Eight independent runs put Jev at **0.753–0.840, median 0.809**: OmarMujahid 0.815, sanand0 0.753, JevBench 0.803 (first-party by its own rules), dopeCape 0.840, PavelRavvich 0.818, cocodedk 0.779, GaNotchVFX 0.788 and Kumzha 0.815 ([github_evaluations audit](https://github.com/OmarMujahid/jev-decision-bench); [sanand0 summary](https://github.com/sanand0/llmevals/blob/main/jev/summary.json); [PavelRavvich](https://github.com/PavelRavvich/jev-bench); [dopeCape](https://github.com/dopeCape/typesafe-ai-test)). Aitejiu's full 3,080-item test split (80.3%) and synthorai's 308 items (80.2%) replicate the centre ([aitejiu](https://dev.to/aitejiu/benchmarking-jev-what-a-decision-model-can-and-cant-do-in-an-agent-harness-20po); [synthorai](https://dev.to/synthorai/jev-vs-flash-llms-7x-cheaper-on-workflows-same-cost-on-one-label-1e2j)). In every study that ran LLMs beside it, the best of them was ahead: sanand0 ranks Jev last of nine, PavelRavvich has GPT-6 Luna at 85.2, GaNotchVFX has Astra at 86.7. **Supervised baselines beat zero-shot Jev wherever one was run**: a trained encoder scores 0.933 on Banking77 ([ickma2311](https://github.com/ickma2311/jev-baselines-eval)), fine-tuned models lead by 2–15 points on five full public splits ([thisisandreeeee](https://github.com/thisisandreeeee/jev-benchmarks)), a 310M Japanese encoder trained on 200 rows beats Jev by 12 points on news topic and ties it on two sentiment tasks ([ikkun1222](https://dev.to/ikkun1222/jev-vs-a-310m-encoder-i-trained-myself-750-rows-three-tasks-two-different-winners-242e)), and logistic regression reaches AUC 0.84 against Jev's 0.77 on CDC heart-risk data ([rubinagentagi-tech](https://github.com/rubinagentagi-tech/jev-heart-risk-bench)).

Jev does well on binary, few-class and ranking tasks. On 18,514 emails it scores 98.33% against TF-IDF logistic regression at 98.39%, and on out-of-distribution mail it holds up where the trained baseline collapses (Ling-Spam 98.57% vs 72.98%; 2026 mailing-list mail 97.3% vs 72.5%), though its prompt wording was tuned on labelled errors ([bitnovus](https://github.com/bitnovus/jev-spam-eval)). It ties dedicated rerankers on eight public retrieval sets (nDCG@10 0.692 vs Cohere Rerank 4 Pro 0.691, [anessbelbati](https://github.com/anessbelbati/jev-rerank-bench)). By question type in Bespoke's 13-dataset suite, Jev leads Nimble on yes/no (84.6% vs 80.2%) and trails on rating scales (50.1% vs 54.6%) ([Nimble](https://github.com/bespokelabsai/nimble/blob/38edc3b576f13179df785d412621d5cb1128d02d/docs/PUBLIC_BENCHMARKS.md)).

The most favourable independent result needs the most care. OmarMujahid's 49-task benchmark re-scores exactly and its gold labels rebuild, but under its own ±0.005 tie rule the count is **29 leads, 14 ties, 6 trails** against a GPT-5.6 Luna deliberately held at reasoning "none" and "low"; only six Jev leads have non-overlapping 95% intervals, and they fall on the most exposed public benchmarks (ARC 0.975, HellaSwag 0.95, TruthfulQA 0.95, WinoGrande 0.885). Its one contamination control, freshly generated maths, went to Luna (0.80 vs 0.75) ([report.json](https://github.com/OmarMujahid/jev-decision-bench/blob/main/report.json)). The pattern fits training-set exposure without proving it; TypeSafe's training data is private, and the only post-cutoff public tests (a June 2026 Turkish entrance exam at 82.3% overall and 17.5% in maths, [yks-bench](https://github.com/UgurcanAkkok/yks-bench); 2026 mail in bitnovus) are small or narrow.

## Jev's probabilities rank well in places and need local fitting everywhere

TypeSafe publishes **no expected calibration error, reliability diagram, Brier score or log loss for Jev on any dataset**; its calibration statements are definitions and training targets, and its only confidence-accuracy figure is one cookbook of 60 SEC filings, 90% right at confidence ≥0.9 and 40% below ([TypeSafe cookbook](https://docs.typesafe.ai/cookbooks/classification_using_confidence)). Its own jaggedness page concedes that "`jev-1.13`'s score levels are weak in numerical calibration" ([Jev 1.13 jaggedness](https://docs.typesafe.ai/model-jaggedness/jev-1.13)). Everything known comes from third parties.

Raw calibration depends on the data and on who Jev is compared with. Against LLMs that write their confidence as a number, Jev's native probabilities often win: its median ECE of 0.157 on the social-science tasks beats 16 of 19 LLMs' stated confidence, and OmarMujahid's mean ECE of 0.070 beats Luna's 0.178 ([2609.24574](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.24574); [OmarMujahid](https://github.com/OmarMujahid/jev-decision-bench)). Once each model gets one fitted temperature, the advantage disappears: Jev's pilot-fitted temperature of **2.661** brings its ECE to 0.121, and then 15 LLMs are lower. Where LLMs were asked for full probability distributions, Jev is at or near the worst: ECE 0.161 in Janardhan's run, highest of six (Fable 0.064); 0.138 in sanand0's, highest of nine; 0.154 on phishing against Haiku's 0.097 ([Janardhan](https://github.com/manjunathshiva/jev-frontier-bench); [sanand0](https://github.com/sanand0/llmevals/blob/main/jev/summary.json); [phishing](https://github.com/anisselbd/jev-phishing-bench/blob/1d56e8c64d029a9554a0874e2ef2901ed196e230/results/report.md)). On familiar English classification, Bespoke's suite finds Jev ECE of 0.038–0.075 on 8 of 10 yes/no and choice sets, lower than Nimble's on 11 of 13, though Nimble ran at temperature 1.0 after its authors had found it overconfident ([Nimble](https://github.com/bespokelabsai/nimble/blob/38edc3b576f13179df785d412621d5cb1128d02d/docs/PUBLIC_BENCHMARKS.md)).

The direction of miscalibration flips with the domain, and no single correction fits every task. On public human-labelled multi-class data Jev is overconfident: Banking77's 0.9–1.0 bin has mean confidence 0.98 and accuracy 92.8%, and the 0.5–0.6 bin 0.55 and 40.2% ([aitejiu report](https://github.com/Aitejiu/jev-harness-lab/blob/29ddf8210e3a96f9d383e9b479e3e5c39455c394/eval/results/banking77-intent-report.md)); on GoEmotions an emotion Jev scored 0.80–0.95 matched the human label 15% of the time ([synthorai](https://dev.to/synthorai/jev-vs-flash-llms-7x-cheaper-on-workflows-same-cost-on-one-label-1e2j)). On crash narratives and author-built synthetic items it is under-confident: calibration slope 1.63 with prevalence overstated (4.8% mean probability against a 2.7% base rate) ([2609.24052](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.24052)), and SamuelSacco's per-tier Platt fits give slopes of 2.11–2.61, "log-odds about half as extreme as they should be" ([issue #9](https://github.com/SamuelSacco/jev-exploration/issues/9)). The judge study's refit temperatures run 0.651, 2.148 and 4.452 across three benchmarks, and the authors conclude "No single temperature fits" ([2609.26550](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.26550)).

A few labels fix most of this. Out-of-fold Platt scaling cut Jev's crash-narrative ECE 3.35x, to 0.0069; a two-parameter refit on civil_comments brought ECE from 0.16–0.21 to under 0.025 on a held-out half ([Adilmp](https://github.com/Adilmp/does-jev-confidence-mean-anything)); transferring a fitted slope and refitting only the intercept on 50 labels cut mean ECE 74% ([SamuelSacco #9](https://github.com/SamuelSacco/jev-exploration/issues/9)). SamuelSacco's four-tier difficulty test (800 synthetic items, 3 passes, labels committed before any call, re-run offline for this review) found one fixed curve compressed toward the middle in every tier, with ECE 0.115–0.147 and precision 1.000 at p ≥ 0.9 in all tiers ([issue #1](https://github.com/SamuelSacco/jev-exploration/issues/1)). The cross-domain version of that test is built and not run.

Ranking quality varies more than the working synthesis states. AUROC for separating right from wrong answers runs from 0.998 on spam ([bitnovus](https://github.com/bitnovus/jev-spam-eval)) and 0.97–0.99 in several binary studies ([Janardhan](https://github.com/manjunathshiva/jev-frontier-bench); [synthorai](https://dev.to/synthorai/jev-vs-flash-llms-7x-cheaper-on-workflows-same-cost-on-one-label-1e2j); [LessWrong trusted-monitor post](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/d7pQicW8EhpPBDRqz/a-non-generative-model-as-a-trusted-monitor-for-ai-control)) down to 0.811 on Banking77 ([sanand0](https://github.com/sanand0/llmevals/blob/main/jev/summary.json)), 0.77 on heart risk, 0.689 on the phishing single verdict ([phishing](https://github.com/anisselbd/jev-phishing-bench)) and 0.56 on Who&When agent-failure attribution ([aitejiu](https://github.com/Aitejiu/jev-harness-lab/tree/29ddf8210e3a96f9d383e9b479e3e5c39455c394)).

Two API properties distort cross-study comparison. The direct API rounds every probability to 0.01 without saying so: across 71 committed responses, 70.4% of Choice probabilities are exactly 0 and 20.4% exactly 1, and Noul is clamped to 0.01–0.98 ([SamuelSacco #10](https://github.com/SamuelSacco/jev-exploration/issues/10)). The crash study found no exact zero in 2.4 million answers, which puts a floor of 0.0046–0.0057 under ECE for rare variables ([2609.24052](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.24052)). Binning conventions move published ECEs substantially: right-closed against left-closed bins turn themsquared's 0.0712 into 0.0488, and at n = 60 a perfectly calibrated model scores about 0.045 ([SamuelSacco #2](https://github.com/SamuelSacco/jev-exploration/issues/2)).

## Speed and cost gains run from parity to 478x depending on what is timed and priced

Jev's service time is short: about 105 ms at the server in OmarMujahid's run, and about 76 ms once the 163 ms network round trip is subtracted from the phishing benchmark's 239 ms median from France ([SamuelSacco #4](https://github.com/SamuelSacco/jev-exploration/issues/4)). Client-side medians run from 0.24 s (France) and 0.43 s (India via OpenRouter) to about 1 s in OmarMujahid's run and 1.9–2.4 s in ikkun1222's; the PR-reported DiffusionGemma alternative serves single reads in 0.12 s on a DGX Spark ([PR #57250](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/pull/57250)). How that converts into a multiple depends on the comparator's reasoning setting, the network path and whether the clock runs on the server or the client. The cost gap comes mostly from free output tokens: in devopsdaily's workload 83% of the old bill was output, and per input token the two prices differed by only 1.31x ([devopsdaily](https://dev.to/devopsdaily/we-measured-the-200x-claim-and-got-it-wrong-twice-first-5ch5)). Where a request packs many questions against one state, billing units change the result, which is the likely reason the two UTS papers disagree: one finds Jev's fees 69–70% below DeepSeek's, the other finds them 1.68–1.72x higher, with the same comparator and provider ([2609.22753](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.22753); [2609.23136](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.23136)). Whether that price is sustainable is unknown, and TypeSafe contradicts itself: the launch post says "We can't prove it isn't subsidized", while the home-page FAQ says "We can serve Jev profitably at our current prices" ([launch post](https://typesafe.ai/blog/introducing-system-one-models-and-jev); [typesafe.ai](https://typesafe.ai/)). No model size, hardware or serving cost is disclosed.

## Failure modes cluster around wording, language and answers absent from the input

The best-controlled robustness result concerns option names. With question, state, rubric text and option set byte-identical, swapping which rubric sits behind "no" and "yes" **changes 32.5% of Jev's answers** against about 2% under neutral names (difference 30.4 points, 95% CI 27.6–33.3; 24x Jev's 1.33% repeat noise), and AUROC falls from 0.81 to 0.58 ([2609.26758](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.26758)). The paper's abstract figures, "70.4 more answers per hundred" and AUC 0.94 → 0.23, belong to an open ModernBERT marker head matching Laya's architecture. No generative LLM was given the same swap, the dataset is unnamed (its four predicates match the synthetic `LocalLLaMA/typed-decisions` set, whose gold comes from a ~4B teacher model), no Jev version is stated and the promised code release has no URL, so the result grades C as evidence about Jev despite the clean design.

Order and wording also move answers. Reversing candidate order changed 3.25% of RewardBench and 11.14% of JudgeBench decisions, and when the rejected answer is the more elaborate one Jev loses 9.2 points while GPT-6 Astra gains 1.3 ([2609.26550](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.26550)). Four phrasings of the same phishing verdict scored 58.4–63.2% with ECE 0.115–0.241, and 2.2% of labels flipped between two identical passes ([phishing](https://github.com/anisselbd/jev-phishing-bench)). Rewording one exclusion rule on 2,000 crash narratives cut pursuit positives from 4 to 1 ([2609.24052](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.24052)). A router given bare option labels sent all 40 hard tasks to the cheap model at confidences of 0.56–1.00 (median 0.96); one-line option descriptions fixed 37 of 40 ([kunko judge-audit](https://github.com/kunko-ai-labs/judge-audit/blob/665ee4f542b18b1c45daf669857d8472161dc8a7/docs/arena-2026-09.md)). Against these, the social-science study found no option-position bias, and the judge study found 0 of 96 repeats changed.

Language shift costs accuracy and calibration. Russian XNLI drops from 88.3% to 77.3% with ECE tripling from 0.032 to 0.096, while intent and topic tasks hold ([AHTOOOXA](https://github.com/AHTOOOXA/jev-cyrillic-audit)); Spanish costs 3–6 points and roughly doubles ECE on XNLI and PAWS-X ([marcosmartinez](https://github.com/marcosmartinez/jev-acento)); Korean holds parity on Belebele and PAWS-X ([mahlernim](https://github.com/mahlernim/jev-korean-benchmark)); German costs 0.5 points on MASSIVE ([Nimble](https://github.com/bespokelabsai/nimble/blob/38edc3b576f13179df785d412621d5cb1128d02d/docs/PUBLIC_BENCHMARKS.md)). TypeSafe's Models page says English is the primary training language ([Models](https://docs.typesafe.ai/models)).

Jev is most confidently wrong when the answer does not follow from the input. On KoBBQ bias questions with no "unknown" option it scores 0.000 accuracy at ECE 0.793, picking the stereotype at 0.79 confidence ([jujumilk3](https://github.com/jujumilk3/jev-calibration-audit)); on genuinely random outcomes it reports 83% where the true rate is 17% ([KantaHayashiAI](https://github.com/KantaHayashiAI/jev-does-not-play-dice)); on heart-risk prediction its probabilities run about 3x too high ([rubinagentagi-tech](https://github.com/rubinagentagi-tech/jev-heart-risk-bench)); on empathy labelling 78% of items land at confidence ≥0.9 with accuracy 0.383 against a 0.371 base rate ([2609.24574](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.24574)); and on ChaosNLI its distribution matched the 100-annotator human split worse than a flat one-third guess (Jensen-Shannon 0.149 vs 0.127) ([Janardhan](https://github.com/manjunathshiva/jev-frontier-bench)). Questions batched in one request cannot see each other ([SamuelSacco PR #11](https://github.com/SamuelSacco/jev-exploration/pull/11)), which explains devopsdaily's case of Jev recommending suspension for an account it classified, in the same response, as a developer sending tests. TypeSafe's own list of weaknesses names literal reading, arithmetic and counting, dates, long irrelevant state, adversarial content and phrasing sensitivity, with examples where "refund" scored 0.72 and "not a refund" 0.47 ([Jev 1.13 jaggedness](https://docs.typesafe.ai/model-jaggedness/jev-1.13)).

Adversarial robustness looks reasonable in the one careful study: as a trusted monitor for backdoored code Jev reached AUROC 0.976 and 0.970 with about 90% of backdoors caught at 2% false positives, and a best-of-5 attack cut that catch rate to 60% ([LessWrong](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/d7pQicW8EhpPBDRqz/a-non-generative-model-as-a-trusted-monitor-for-ai-control)). No study has measured Jev's answers changing over time; the one post that claimed drift produced its failing example by editing an expected value by hand ([jev-watch README](https://github.com/akanthed/jev-watch/blob/a0ddd8fac62b2b5f7e7cfb1c6500de34de95c6ea/README.md)). Every pinned independent run used `jev-1.13.0`; `jev-1.12`, pinned in 16 of TypeSafe's own cookbooks, was reported gone by 2026-09-17, and no model changelog exists ([Models](https://docs.typesafe.ai/models); [cequence-io client](https://github.com/cequence-io/openai-scala-client/blob/master/typesafe-client/src/main/scala/io/cequence/openaiscala/typesafe/domain/TypeSafeModelId.scala)).

## TypeSafe's own claims range from sourced to unfalsifiable

**The 193.6x and 444.6x multiples.** TypeSafe's launch post names the source as its four in-house workflow evals and calls the figures "on the higher end of real world gains" ([launch post](https://typesafe.ai/blog/introducing-system-one-models-and-jev)). It never names the comparator. Checked in a script against the rounded summary points on [evals.typesafe.ai](https://evals.typesafe.ai/), 444.6x fits only Jev against Opus 5 in the workflow setup; 193.6x fits Sonnet 5 workflow, DeepSeek v4 Pro workflow, Opus 5 prompt-only, or the mean of the eight prompt-only setups. Averaged over all eight workflow setups the multiples are **97.8x faster and 149.2x cheaper**. The LLMs ran through TypeSafe's own adapter, which asks them for probabilities and which TypeSafe says makes them "slower and more expensive"; timings came from "our laptops on the West Coast"; no case counts, run counts, variance or Jev version are published. TypeSafe's speed claims also disagree across its own launch-day releases: under 100 ms and "up to 100 times" (press release), 20–200x faster and 40–400x cheaper (founder's X post), 70–500 ms and 40–200x (launch post), and 193.6x/444.6x (home page) ([Business Wire](https://finance.yahoo.com/technology/ai/articles/typesafe-ai-emerges-stealth-40m-190000776.html); [X](https://x.com/CompleteSkeptic/status/2099925682726002904)).

**What the eval accuracy means.** Reference labels on the workflow evals are "an average of the responses of GPT-6 Astra and Claude Fable 5.1, both at high thinking" ([evals.typesafe.ai](https://evals.typesafe.ai/)), which the CEO confirmed on Hacker News ([HN 49719816](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49719816)). Accuracy there is agreement with two LLMs. On that measure Jev scores 67.8%, equal to Sonnet 5 and 0.1 below GPT-5.6 Terra, with Sol leading at 74.1%; on invoice processing Jev scores 61.8% against Sol's 79.1%, beaten by every setup except Haiku 4.5. TypeSafe states that it "deliberately chose *not* to publish performance against *public* benchmarks" ([launch post](https://typesafe.ai/blog/introducing-system-one-models-and-jev)), so no vendor-published public-dataset result exists.

**"0% hallucination".** TypeSafe's launch post says: "Our number is not empirical. Schema matching is guaranteed, thus we can confidently add 0% into the plots." The home-page FAQ adds that Jev "can choose the wrong one" ([typesafe.ai](https://typesafe.ai/)). Independent data confirms the schema guarantee (zero invalid answers across 23,703 decision-model calls in [2609.24574](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.24574)) and shows the wrong-valid-answer rate elsewhere in this review. The CEO conceded on HN that Jev can be "confidently wrong" ([HN 49718780](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49718780)), while a TypeSafe engineer in the same thread said it "will never produce unreliable outputs" ([HN 49720154](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49720154)). **RLCD.** No paper, patent, loss definition or method post exists; the arXiv API returns nothing for RLCD after 2026-09-01, and the only arXiv "RLCD" is an unrelated 2023 method, Reinforcement Learning from Contrastive Distillation ([arXiv 2307.12950](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.12950)). TypeSafe describes it only by its objective, "answers with epistemically honest probabilities on System One tasks" ([launch post](https://typesafe.ai/blog/introducing-system-one-models-and-jev)). On Latent Space the founder called it "this new task … another North Star" rather than an algorithm, and Latent Space itself calls it "a novel, unpublished technique" ([Latent Space](https://www.latent.space/p/jev)). On HN he said the architecture is "close to the chest for now, but we have talked about writing a paper" ([HN 49718824](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49718824)). TechCrunch expands the acronym as "from calibrated decisions"; TypeSafe's pages say "for".

**Training data and architecture.** TypeSafe says "We make all the data ourselves"; asked on Latent Space whether all its data is synthetic, the founder answered "Yep" ([Latent Space](https://www.latent.space/p/jev)). TechCrunch's widely repeated "trained exclusively on synthetic data" is the reporter's paraphrase, outside quotation marks ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/18/a-new-kind-of-ai-model-from-a-chatgpt-inventor-is-thrilling-developers/)). Size, base model and attention design are undisclosed. The launch FAQ says Jev "is neither small nor an LLM", TechCrunch reports unnamed observers suspect an open-weight LLM base, and the founder says he "wouldn't pre-train" even with a billion dollars, which together fit post-training on a pretrained base without confirming it. An HN commenter's claim that Jev "consistently thinks it's Qwen" is an unsupported allegation ([HN 49809071](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49809071)). The credential holds under that narrower definition.

**Company.** TypeSafe was founded in 2024 in San Francisco and emerged on 2026-09-15 with a $40M seed round led by DCVC; co-founders are Almeida (CEO), Sasha Sheng (COO) and Erik Gafni (CTO) ([Business Wire](https://finance.yahoo.com/technology/ai/articles/typesafe-ai-emerges-stealth-40m-190000776.html); [team page](https://typesafe.ai/team)). TypeSafe names no other investor, no headcount and no customer; the users named in press (Vercel, Bryo AI, Earendil) come from TechCrunch, whose Vercel figure ("five to 18 times more quickly and with greater accuracy") appears nowhere with data. ## Every piece of the mechanism predates Jev

Jev's observable mechanism, ending the prompt where the answer starts and taking a softmax over only the allowed label tokens, is the default multiple-choice method of the last six years. GPT-3 §2.4 compared "the LM likelihood of each completion" and gave binary options "semantically meaningful names (e.g. 'True' or 'False' rather than 0 or 1)" ([arXiv 2005.14165](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.14165)); MMLU §4.1 read the probabilities of "A"–"D" and §4.2 found zero-shot GPT-3 "uncalibrated" by up to 24 points ([arXiv 2009.03300](https://arxiv.org/abs/2009.03300)); lm-evaluation-harness still scores MMLU this way through `output_type: multiple_choice` ([task guide](https://github.com/EleutherAI/lm-evaluation-harness/blob/main/docs/task_guide.md)). The verbalizer, a map from each label to one vocabulary token scored at a mask, was introduced by Schick and Schütze ([arXiv 2001.07676](https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.07676)) and extended to multi-token labels a year later ([arXiv 2009.07118](https://arxiv.org/abs/2009.07118)). Zero-shot classification as entailment dates to Yin, Hay and Roth ([arXiv 1909.00161](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.00161)). "Calibrate Before Use" showed that label probabilities read from an LM carry majority, recency and common-token bias and corrected it with a content-free input; that correction targets accuracy and variance rather than ECE ([arXiv 2102.09690](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.09690)). The option-name sensitivity measured in 2609.26758 belongs to this documented family.

The calibration story is equally established. Guo et al. showed that modern networks are overconfident and that a single temperature fitted on held-out data fixes most of it without changing any prediction ([arXiv 1706.04599](https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.04599)). The log score, which cross-entropy training minimises, is strictly proper ([Gneiting & Raftery 2007](https://sites.stat.washington.edu/raftery/Research/PDF/Gneiting2007jasa.pdf)), so "a reward that is a proper scoring rule" is ordinary maximum-likelihood training in RL vocabulary; Guo §3 is the caveat that such training still miscalibrates on held-out data. The GPT-4 report, on which Almeida is an author, documents post-training raising MMLU ECE from 0.007 to 0.074 ([arXiv 2303.08774](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.08774)), and Kadavath et al. found one temperature of 2.5 "largely fixes" RLHF policies' miscalibration ([arXiv 2207.05221](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.05221)). The fitted temperatures reported for Jev (2.66 on social-science tasks) and for the open replicas (Kev about 2.1–2.4, Nimble 2.179, Luce 2.5–3.3 on unseen tasks) fall in the same range. The only public description of an RLCD-style objective is Laya's: Gaussian noise on the logits, a proper-scoring-rule reward and REINFORCE with a group-mean baseline ([Laya card](https://huggingface.co/convaiinnovations/laya)). Cascades that answer when confident and defer otherwise have a formal treatment in selective classification ([arXiv 1705.08500](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.08500)) and an LLM-cost version in FrugalGPT ([arXiv 2305.05176](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.05176)); several Jev cascade results (Janardhan's 82.5% at 37% of Fable's cost, REFLEX's τ = 0.5) chose their thresholds on the test data, the discipline Geifman and El-Yaniv's method supplies.

What is new is packaging: a typed API (`choice`, `score`, `noul`), a hosted endpoint with output tokens free, parallel evaluation of many questions against one ingested state, and a model tuned by undisclosed means for this use. Whether TypeSafe's training differs materially from cross-entropy plus a fitted temperature cannot be judged until RLCD is published.

Decomposition helps both models about equally.

## Conflicts of interest run through most of the favourable coverage

The studies with no identifiable stake (Ibrahim and Zaki, Rafe and Das, Janardhan, sanand0, SamuelSacco, OmarMujahid) produce the least favourable accuracy results or the most qualified favourable ones. Favourable figures come disproportionately from parties with a stake. Every's "Mini-Vibe Check" was written by a launch partner with day-one access; its accuracy evidence is Dan Shipper's 6 of 7 planted defects caught against Fable's 7 of 7 on 12 synthetic passages ([Every](https://every.to/vibe-check/mini-vibe-check-typesafe-s-jev-judged-everything-i-ve-written-in-0-7-seconds)). Near Here had early access and scored against labels "written by the assistant" ([Near Here](https://nearhere.events/blog/typesafe-jev-mistral-gemini-event-validation)).

Undisclosed interests appear on both sides. synthorai, whose post finds Jev below flash LLMs on accuracy, runs an LLM gateway selling access to the rival models and timed them through "our proxy" without saying so in the post ([synthorai.io](https://synthorai.io/)). heiko-hotz's SNIPS study, where Jev narrowly trails Gemini Flash-Lite, is by a Google employee comparing against Google's Gemini and Gemma, undisclosed in the repo ([heiko-hotz](https://github.com/heiko-hotz/jev-evaluation)). One hazard sits outside the studies. `thejevai.com`, registered 2026-09-20 and promoted on Hugging Face by an account named `sora-2` as the endpoint for API keys, belongs to an unidentified party; TypeSafe's API is `api.typesafe.ai` ([HF post](https://huggingface.co/blog/sora-2/how-to-use-the-jev-ai-model-a-step-by-step-develop)). Keys should not be sent there.

### Where this review departs from the working synthesis

Four statements in `jev/NOTES.md` go further than the primary-source audits support, and this review follows the audits. First, NOTES says grade-A studies show ranking AUC of 0.97–0.99; the audited range is 0.56–0.998, with 0.97–0.99 holding for binary and spam tasks and 0.69–0.81 for the phishing verdict, Banking77 and aligned workflow decisions. Second, NOTES puts speed and cost at "2–15x faster and 6–478x cheaper"; client wall-clock runs show 1.04x against a reasoning-off LLM, Jev slower than a local Gemma 4 26B and than Luna on long contexts, cost near parity with DeepSeek on Banking77 (1.18x), and Jev dearer in one edge-orchestration paper (1.7x) and against a 0.6B reranker (about 4x, [denser-org](https://github.com/denser-org/rerank-bench-jev)). Third, NOTES says human-labelled studies put Jev's calibration "at or near the worst"; that holds against LLMs that return distributions (Janardhan, sanand0, phishing), while against verbalised LLM confidence Jev's raw ECE is better than most (2609.24574, OmarMujahid), and on crash narratives it sits between Fable and Sol. Fourth, NOTES lists two Gemma data points; the audits contain at least five, set out in the next section. NOTES also describes sanand0's result as "cheapest after DeepSeek"; the source's figures make Jev the cheapest ($0.0717 per 1,000) with DeepSeek next ($0.0849).

| [kunko judge-audit](https://github.com/kunko-ai-labs/judge-audit/blob/665ee4f542b18b1c45daf669857d8472161dc8a7/docs/arena-2026-09.md) | Gemma 4 E4B on Ollama, confidence written in JSON | synthetic emails and routing | adversarial emails 81.0% vs 95.5%; bare-label router sent 28/40 hard tasks to the strong model vs Jev's 0/40 | B |

Three conclusions follow. Gemma 4 31B, prompted generatively, is at least competitive with Jev on human-labelled accuracy in the one grade-A comparison, at higher cost and latency. The generative read-out is fragile in ways a label-logit read-out removes: heiko-hotz's 18-point swing from code fences and kunko's verbalised-confidence router both measure output parsing as much as judgement. And DiffusionGemma's favourable showing rests on a single author-built, single-run set with no published items, contradicted by ywchiu's independent (synthetic, grade B) result where the DiffusionGemma-based `djev-spark` scored 32.2% against Jev's 61.4%. Google's Gemma account describes bidirectional attention as "yielding well-calibrated decision distributions" without data ([Google Gemma on X](https://x.com/googlegemma/status/2101069861598482817), not re-checked in this review); no calibration metric for DiffusionGemma exists in the PR, the thread or anywhere else found. SimpleJev serves a Gemma 4 26B-A4B classifier by exactly the label-logit method but publishes no accuracy and calls its probabilities uncalibrated ([SimpleJev](https://github.com/featherless-ai/simple-jev)).

Beyond Gemma, the following remain unmeasured as of 2026-09-23. Jev's behaviour across model versions, since every independent run used `jev-1.13.0` and no changelog exists. Accuracy on data guaranteed to postdate Jev's training, whose cutoff is unpublished. Calibration across difficulty on real rather than synthetic items, and SamuelSacco's built but unrun cross-domain transfer test. Whether a generative LLM shows the same option-name sensitivity as Jev. Nimble on the 13-set public suite at its fitted temperature. Any independent measurement of Kev, SimpleJev or the vLLM PR. TypeSafe's workflow evals' case counts, run counts and variance. RLCD's method, Jev's size and base, and the cost of serving it. The two-arm experiment in this project (`jev/run_eval.py`: DiffusionGemma one-step reads against Gemma 4 26B next-token label logits on 1,200 public labelled items, with calibration, Brier, log loss and noise-draw spread) is built and unit-tested but has not been run on a model.

## Conclusion

The evidence moves Jev from "a new kind of model" to a well-served instance of an old technique: label-token scoring from a post-trained transformer, sold with free output and a typed interface. That reframing matters for decisions. A team choosing Jev is buying latency, price and operational simplicity for binary and few-class judgements, and accepting mid-tier accuracy on nuanced multi-class work, probabilities whose direction of error changes by domain, sensitivity to option wording and language, and a vendor that has published neither its training method nor a single calibration metric. The studies agree on the remedy: label a few hundred of the task's own decisions, fit a temperature or a two-parameter map on them, choose thresholds on held-out data, and measure an option-swap flip rate beside accuracy. With those labels in hand, the same recipe applies to any open model, which is why the open replicas matched or beat Jev as soon as they trained on task data.

The unsettled question is whether an open model read the same way needs the vendor at all. For Gemma the answer is one experiment away and has not been published: plain Gemma 4 read by label logits, on human-labelled public data plus private post-cutoff decisions, with a temperature fitted on 0–300 labels and scored for calibration and name-swap sensitivity. The generative Gemma 4 31B already rivals Jev on the one grade-A comparison; removing output parsing should help, and whether its label probabilities need less refitting than Jev's is the measurement that would decide the build-or-buy case.
